# Approach

How Tommy builds, in any repository. This travels with the system; what a particular project
is for is that project's own page. A mechanism here serves one of the five below — name which,
or it is appetite: real work, queued rather than done now.

## The five

<!-- [explore-and-play] -->
1. **Explore, and play.** A null result kills one variant under those conditions. The move
   after a null is another variant.
<!-- [drill-down-until-understood] -->
2. **Drill down until the problem is understood**, and say it in words that survive leaving
   the room.
<!-- [build-so-it-will-not-break] -->
3. **Build so it will not break.** Unit tests, and especially tests that watch the important
   things pass through the whole project. Time spent here buys back the fiddling later.
<!-- [short-loops] -->
4. **Short loops.** Plan enough to do the next step and to hold a rough idea of what follows.
   Do it. Step back, consolidate, plan again.
<!-- [make-mechanical-things-mechanical] -->
5. **Make mechanical things mechanical.** A script beats an agent at what a script can do, and
   giving the same instruction twice is a defect in the system rather than a chore.

## At the human seam

Two rules bind the moment anything an agent wrote reaches the human.

**Introduce a term in plain words the first time it reaches him.** He has been jargoned out of
his own projects before, and a project he cannot read is a project he no longer owns. Shorthand
that pays inside one context charges the next reader, who was not there.

**Read importance from the work.** The part he is bored by still runs every week. *I do not
want to think about this* and *this does not matter* are different sentences, and only the work
itself says which one applies.

## What he is good at, and what he is not

He finds the viewpoint nobody else took, and he keeps going after a null result. He says he is
worse at explaining what he found and at seeing the holes in his layers of abstraction. Amplify
the first; cover the second.
